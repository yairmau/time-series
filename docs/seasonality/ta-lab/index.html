Fit and data settings

Classical additive decomposition on the full 2020–2022 record, with a centered 365-day moving-mean trend (linearly extended at the ends). The mean annual pattern is then smoothed across 15, 31, or 61 days, wrapping across the cycle boundary. This keeps individual weather fluctuations out of the repeating baseline. Select a saved smoothing setting; zoom changes the view, not the fit. The 365-sample annual period approximates the calendar across the leap year. Daily averaging removes much of the within-day cycle; it does not remove the annual cycle.

Fit and data settings

Feuer et al. (2025), Zenodo release v1.0.1. Half-hourly circumference measurements are converted to diameter change (divide by π, then subtract the initial value) and averaged hourly. No gaps are filled. Exact STL fits use period 24, trend span 49 hours, and robust fitting. The seasonal span counts days in each hour's subseries. Fits use both years; zooming does not refit. “Fixed” uses classical additive decomposition.

Trend and data settings

NOAA ITRDB CA533: Graybill and LaMarche, Campito Mountain, bristlecone pine. One core from each of 12 tree IDs; original units 0.01 mm, converted to mm. Gaps retain their calendar position and zero-width rings remain zero. The trend is an ordinary centered rolling mean over years, fitted on each full record. Available observations are averaged equally at the edges and around gaps. Each index is width divided by a positive trend. The chronology is Tukey’s biweight robust mean (C = 9) across the displayed trees in each year, with missing indices omitted. Its sample depth is the number of available trees. No prewhitening or variance stabilization is applied. Short windows can remove shared variation as well as age-related growth. Formal dendrochronology also uses negative exponential curves and splines.
